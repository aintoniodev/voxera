The Evolutionary Loop

Every evolutionary algorithm is the same four-step loop. Only the slots change: what an individual is, what counts as variation, what measures fitness, and how selection keeps or discards. Print this page; it is the map for the whole course.

The loop

1 · PopulationA set of individuals — candidate solutions
▼
2 · VariationMutation (one parent → one child) & crossover (two parents → children)
▼
3 · EvaluationFitness function scores each individual
▼
4 · Selection & heredityFitter individuals survive and reproduce; the rest are discarded
▲ repeat ▲
0 · RepeatWinners become the parents of the next generation
The one-line summary

Evolution = variation (make new candidates from old ones) + selection (keep the better ones) + heredity (what you keep is what you build from next). Everything else — trees, networks, architectures, code — is a choice of what the individuals are.

Glossary

Individual / candidate
One solution in the population — a program, a network, a weight vector, a commit of training code.
Population
The set of individuals alive at one time. Size is the main knob between "careful hill-climbing" (small) and "broad exploration" (large).
Generation
One pass through the loop: vary → evaluate → select → the new population.
Fitness function
The objective score of an individual. Must be cheap enough to call many times — it is the bottleneck of almost every evolutionary system.
Variation / variation operators
Anything that produces a new individual from old ones. Two classics: mutation (one parent → one child, small random change) and crossover / recombination (two parents → children, mixing parts). In LLM-based systems the variation operator is a prompted language model.
Selection
The rule deciding who survives and who reproduces. Tournament selection (pick the best of k random), elitism (always keep the top few), fitness-proportionate, rank-based.
Heredity
The fact that survivors' traits are inherited by the next generation. Without heredity you have random search; without selection you have random drift.
Elitism
Copying the best individual(s) unchanged into the next generation, so the best-so-far is never lost.
(1+1)-ES
Population of one: mutate the current solution, keep it if better, else revert. Aka hill climbing with a stochastic step. The minimal evolutionary algorithm.
Exploration vs exploitation
Exploration = trying far-flung variants; exploitation = refining what already works. High mutation = explore; low mutation = exploit. Crossover and larger populations add exploration; elitism adds exploitation.
Local optimum
A solution better than its immediate neighbours but worse than the global best. Hill climbing gets stuck here; populations and crossover are the classic escape routes.
Genetic drift
Change in the population from chance, not fitness. With weak selection pressure, drift dominates and progress stalls.
Hall of fame
An archive of the best individuals found so far, kept outside the population (used by FunSearch).
Age / regularized evolution
Penalizing old individuals in selection so young ones get a chance — prevents the search from freezing on an early winner (Real et al. 2019).
Innovation number
NEAT's global counter tagging each structural gene so crossover can align matching parts of two different topologies.
Speciation
Dividing the population into niches of similar individuals, with fitness shared within a niche — protects novel structures long enough to be evaluated (NEAT).
Genotype / phenotype
Genotype = the representation evolution acts on (code, weights, graph); phenotype = what gets evaluated (behavior, trained model). One genotype can map to many phenotypes.
